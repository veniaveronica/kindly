Why Practicing Good Netiquette Really Matters

An original hortatory exposition essay for classroom discussion

Thesis

The internet has quietly become the place where most young people talk, learn, and connect — yet many still treat it as a lawless space where ordinary manners no longer apply. Surely, this cannot continue. We should all take netiquette seriously, because how we behave online quietly shapes the kind of internet we all have to live in.

1️⃣ Argument 1

Firstly, unkind words online cause very real emotional harm. A cruel comment is read the same way a spoken insult is felt — the screen between two people does not soften the impact. Teenagers who experience repeated harassment online often report anxiety and a growing fear of opening their own social media apps. Treating someone with basic respect costs nothing, yet it protects something valuable: another person's peace of mind.

2️⃣ Argument 2

Secondly, whatever is posted online is rarely erased completely, even after it is deleted. Screenshots are taken, messages are forwarded, and a careless comment can resurface years later, at exactly the wrong moment — a college application, a job interview, a new friendship. Clearly, the internet does not forget the way memory does, which makes thinking before posting an essential habit, not an optional one.

3️⃣ Argument 3

Finally, a respectful online culture is simply more useful for everyone. When people focus on ideas instead of defending themselves from personal attacks, group chats stay productive, class discussions stay on-topic, and online communities become places worth returning to. Good etiquette is not about being overly polite — it is about keeping shared digital spaces functional for the people who rely on them.

Recommendation

Therefore, before hitting "send," we should all pause and consider how our words might land on the other side of the screen. Schools and families must teach netiquette the same way they teach face-to-face manners, and every one of us should choose kindness even when a reply button makes cruelty easy. Certainly, a kinder internet starts with each individual decision to type a little more carefully.

Now look back at the essay:
  • Which Argument relies most on emotional appeal, and which relies most on logical reasoning?
  • Find every temporal connective (Firstly, Secondly, Finally) — do they appear in a logical order?
  • Spot two examples of passive voice. Why might the writer have chosen passive over active voice there?
  • If you disagreed with this thesis, what counter-evidence could you raise?
Notice: The essay never orders the reader to act — it persuades through reasoning (emotional harm, permanence, community benefit) and only asks for a change in behavior at the very end. That restraint is what separates hortatory exposition from a simple command.
